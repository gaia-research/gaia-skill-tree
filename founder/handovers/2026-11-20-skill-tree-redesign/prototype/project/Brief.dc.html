BriefLiving Atlas · first design pass19 Nov 2026

The Living Atlas

Living Atlas is this commission's internal codename. It never appears on the site; the explorer is called the Tree Explorer, and its nav label is Explore.

One public record, more than one way to read it. Yggdrasil is the monument and the default world. Arbor is the instrument, entered through a lens. The Observatory is the threshold to the rest of the house.

Creative thesis

The redesign keeps what makes Gaia recognisable (the gold World Tree, EB Garamond, Departure Mono numerals, Honor Red handles, the rank ladders and evidence metals) and changes what had aged: the rainbow navigation, centred stacks of cards and eyebrows, emoji icons, stat-card heroes and one-off page layouts. Every page now shares one shell and one grammar, and each Tree brings its own world into it.

Yggdrasil · monument

Warm obsidian, matched to the Ascension Overdrive so the locked section belongs to the page. Organic curves, gold light on a real DAG: Basic capabilities are the roots, fusions the crown by depth. The approved raster aligns to the live trunk as one silhouette. Serif display, specimen dossiers, pedestals, ledger tables.

Arbor · instrument

Washed charcoal and bone. A 3D circuit tree after the founder's Arbor I concept art: a bundled trunk of traces jogging at 45°, branches on five depth planes ending in ringed terminals, roots running across a ground plane. The front silhouette matches the concept; the planes separate as the camera moves. A framed viewport, a condensed grotesque voice, readouts instead of rankings. Heaven, Hell and Ultra act on the instrument only.

Principles

  1. Projection, not theme. The lens changes geometry, section composition, metrics and voice. Identity, evidence metals, rank, Honor Red and challenge keep one meaning in every Tree.
  2. The ledger precedes the interface. Every number in the prototype comes from the 17 Nov 2026 registry snapshot. Anything illustrative says Prototype or Specimen on its face.
  3. Gold is earned. Apex Gold appears on 5★ and 6★, the seal and the World Tree mark. The GitHub star loses its gold pill.
  4. Unknown is visible. Not measured is dotted, never zero, never hatched (hachure belongs to challenges). Empty panels stay empty rather than estimating.
  5. Plates, not eyebrows. One plate strip per page header carries the atlas register. Sections lead with their heading, not a kicker.
  6. Weather is local. Heaven, Hell and Ultra change the Arbor instrument, never the navigation, text or evidence materials around it.
  7. Locked means locked. The Ascension Overdrive is embedded as a capture of the live section, unchanged.

Design-to-production boundary

All state is local to the artifact.

Production functionality represented

World Tree hero and Explore in 3D, named skills filters, Evidence Library, Trust Ledger and Codex, challenge components, Hall of Heroes, profiles, docs pages, install script. Real snapshot data, rendered in the new system.

New proposals

The lens; Arbor home composition; the Tree Explorer inspector; the specimen dossier; Hell-Heaven stamps; the Observatory; regrouped navigation; Builder and Developer docs; the ledger meter; warm obsidian ground; Unique ink tokens.

Deferred contracts

Arbor projection positions; Hell-Heaven index and stamps; benchmark receipts; Website Experience Manifest; /tree/ routes; TM history snapshots; Blender canon and web anchors; challenge projection source.

Hidden or folded legacy controls

Field view folds into the Tree Explorer. The Named Skills flow view stays, as Skills · Flow. Tile and List become Specimens and Ledger. Per-link nav colours go. The gold star pill becomes an icon. The duplicate legacy Hall block and the "three names" text section give way to the Observatory threshold. Evidence stat cards become the alloy bar. No data, provenance or route is removed.

Hall of Heroes: making it fast

The Hall is the people page, and it is long: 48 stages, each with a photo. The prototype is built the way production should be.

Paint only what is on screen

Each stage carries content-visibility: auto with contain-intrinsic-size: auto 600px, so the browser skips layout and paint for stages off screen while the scrollbar stays honest. One static page background; no per-stage backdrop, blur or fixed attachment, so nothing repaints on scroll.

One observer, no scroll listener

A single IntersectionObserver with a middle-band root margin sets the active plate and marks a stage as seen for its one-time entrance. The rail re-renders only when the active plate changes, and keeps its own list scrolled with scrollTop, never scrollIntoView on the page.

Avatars at the size they are drawn

Request avatars.githubusercontent.com/<handle>?s=480 for the 240 px stage photo and ?s=64 for the rail, each with width, height, loading="lazy" and decoding="async". Preload only the first stage's photo. Keep the monogram fallback for a failed load. The wreath is one shared SVG file, cached once, not inlined 48 times.

Motion that costs nothing

Entrances animate transform and opacity only, run once per stage, and are off under reduced motion. Medallions use the 240 px badge tier, not the 800 px card tier. Data comes from one precomputed hall slice (the plates, sorted), not the full named index.

Yggdrasil in 3D: the explorer rebuilt

The live 3D view keeps its real layout and its stars and black holes. What changes is everything drawn on top of it, how you move through it, and what each frame costs. The board Yggdrasil in 3D runs on the real snapshot: the site's own world-tree-layout.js computed the positions for all 317 skills and 501 links, so the shape matches production exactly. It opens from Explore in 3D on the home hero and from the Tree Explorer.

Moving inside the tree

Orbit

Drag turns the tree around the current point. Scroll or pinch moves closer. Selecting a skill from search, a lineage row or the person list flies the camera there on a 0.7 to 1.7 second ease, and it then orbits that skill.

Fly

Drag looks around without moving. W, A, S, D move, Q and E go down and up, Shift is faster, arrows turn. Edges are clipped at the camera so the wood stays continuous while you pass through it, and nodes fade as they reach the lens instead of filling the screen.

Keys

/ search, R whole tree, F fly to the selection, L labels, Space slow turn, + and − move, Esc clears search, then the selection, then leaves. Focus stays inside the dialog.

Phone

Search moves under the title; the inspector and the lens become bottom sheets, and the tree lifts into the space above them. Drag orbits, two fingers pinch, double tap flies. The Explore in 3D button now shows on phones too.

Two shapes, same explorer

The Yggdrasil in 3D: hero tree shape board networks the skills the way the home hero does, with the explorer's navigation and presentation kept as they are. Basic skills are the roots, grouped by cluster and pulled toward the trunk by how many skills build on them; a root fibre runs from the trunk base to each one. Fusions climb the crown by depth. Each grows on a branch from its deepest prerequisite, or from the trunk when that prerequisite is a Basic. Other fusion-to-fusion prerequisites are quiet grafts. Because the wood shows only the deepest prerequisite, selecting a skill in this shape draws every true prerequisite link as the dashed lineage. The live layout board keeps the site's own positions and colours, and now draws its links the way the hero shape does: a twisted trunk up the layout's axis, a root fibre to every Basic, each fusion on a branch from its structural parent (or from the trunk where its route met it), and every other prerequisite as a quiet strand that bows through the heartwood. The long routed loops are gone. Every node is round: a fusion carries a fine inner ring instead of a diamond. A Review box (bottom right) switches either board between the two shapes, and the tree morphs between them.

Uniques as fruit

There are 31 Uniques now, not three, so they are quieter. They are sized like every other skill of their rank: there is no larger floor for Uniques, and 4★ Uniques match other 4★ skills. Halo, spiral arms, particles and the event horizon are all dimmer, and the 5★ Unique takes the burnished copper of --rank-5-unique. The close-approach view is unchanged: a lensed accretion disk, the brighter approaching side, a photon ring and bent starlight.

Each Unique hangs as fruit from a bough on a thin stem. The bough is a prerequisite in the crown, or failing that a crown skill that builds on it, or failing that a proven bough in its own cluster; no bough carries more than three. That seats all 31 on 24 boughs in both shapes. The inspector names the bough. The Review box's Uniques switch shows the alternative: the spread ring outside the trunk in the live shape, or placement by type in the hero shape.

Layout finding, filed as gaia-skill-tree#2480. The live layout puts all 31 Uniques on six outside anchors, 17 of them on one point, so only six black holes can be seen or clicked. The prototype fixes it at render time; the issue asks for the fix in world-tree-layout.js once the fruit or ring choice is made.

Performance

Measured in the same headless Chromium at 1440 by 900 with software rendering and no GPU, so treat the numbers as a comparison, not a target. Live explorer: 16 frames a second, worst frame 133 ms. Rebuilt: about 60 frames a second; 46 to 47 frames a second during a full-screen black-hole approach. With curved wood in both shapes, the live shape draws in about 2.3 ms a frame and the hero shape in about 1.9 ms (measured while other renders shared the machine). Each wood polyline is projected once per frame into a screen-space cache. For debugging, press ` (or add ?debug=frames) to show draw time, frame rate and quality on any machine.

Paint once, stamp every frame

Glows, coronas, flares, pulsar beams, halos, spiral arms, accretion disks and lens rings are painted once into small canvases when the explorer opens. The frame loop only stamps them with drawImage. The live code builds several radial gradients per node per frame, roughly a thousand allocations a frame.

Batch the wood

Wood is grouped by kind, colour and depth band, so every trunk strand, root fibre, branch and strand draws in at most 132 strokes, whichever shape is showing.

Leave the DOM alone

Panels re-render only when the selection, search or filters change. Per frame, the page writes two transforms (hover tip and altitude marker) and nothing else. Labels are canvas text with cached widths and a collision pass capped at 90.

Adapt, then rest

Resolution steps down while the camera moves and when frames run long, and steps back up when they recover. The loop pauses when the tab is hidden or the explorer is off screen. Under reduced motion nothing animates and frames are drawn only on input.

Asset request: the rendered keystone

Purpose and placement. A rendered 3D version of the Gaia seal for the keystone at the centre of The Observatory dome (about 90 to 160 px wide), and possibly the 28 px nav seal.

Geometry to keep exactly. Two nested squares rotated 45 degrees on one centre: outer side 15.8 and inner side 8.4 on a 28-unit grid. A serif capital G in EB Garamond SemiBold, centred, baseline 3.6 units below centre, size 10.5 units. Front view, at most 8 degrees of tilt.

Material and light. Key light from the upper left, soft fill from the lower right. Bevelled metal frames with a centre ridge; the G raised above the inner square. Yggdrasil: warm gold with parchment highlights and a dark umber recess. Arbor: cooler green-teal metal with a slate recess.

Formats. Transparent WebP at 2x for 160, 96 and 56 px, per world, plus a flat two-tone SVG fallback. No baked background, glow or wordmark. At 28 px both squares and the G stay distinct. Overlays the SVG seal within 1 px at 160 px, reads on both world grounds, under 60 KB a file. Model choice follows .agents/skills/gaia-image-production/SKILL.md.
