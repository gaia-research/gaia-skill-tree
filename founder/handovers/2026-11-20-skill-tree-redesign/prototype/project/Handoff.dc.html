What the prototype needs from other teams

Only assets with a clear role are requested. The prototype stands on approved art today; each packet says what it replaces and how it will be judged.

  • Loop (founder ruling, 20 Nov): Sol generates, Terra critiques against canon and this brief, Sol corrects, Terra verifies. Claude, the cloud design lead, then stamps the asset; the founder may veto. An asset counts only once it is stamped and not vetoed.
  • Team: Sol at Xhigh, solo, in Codex. Model: Codex defaults to gpt-image-2.6, on the founder's attestation, so there is no model preflight; the ledger credit records it. Sol reads the canonical gaia-image-production skill in gaia-research before starting.
  • Masters and ledger entries live in gaia-research; WebP derivatives reach Skill Tree only when the site is wired, which waits for the founder to see the final product. No raster carries text.
  • Repository handoffs: founder/handovers/2026-11-20-skill-tree-redesign/ in gaia-skill-tree, tracked by issue 2481.

Image packets

A1 · Arbor 3D canon and instrument plateNeeded for parity
Destination
The Arbor model in the Blender canon (per the merged multi-tree endgame doc), its still render behind the Arbor hero, and the Tree Explorer in Arbor. The live canvas stays the only semantic layer.
Objective
Build the tree the prototype sketches: a bundled trunk of parallel traces that jogs at 45°, main branches peeling off on five separated depth planes, thin secondary traces with small circle, square and diamond terminals, ringed terminals at branch ends, and roots spreading as traces across a ground plane. Keep the front silhouette of the founder's Arbor I concept; the depth is what the concept does not show.
Prompt for Sol
A tree built from circuit traces, seen from slightly above and in front, standing on a dark ground plane. The trunk is a bundle of six to eight thin parallel traces that turns only at 45 degrees. Branches leave the trunk on several separated depth planes, like sheets of glass stacked front to back: each runs diagonally up at 45 degrees, then horizontally, and ends in a small ring terminal. Thin secondary traces split off with tiny circle, square and diamond pads. Roots spread across the ground plane as traces with 45-degree turns, receding into depth. Washed charcoal ground (#1b1d1b), traces in muted green (#61c674) with bone-white (#eeeae2) highlights at terminals, faint corner brackets marking each depth plane. Crisp, technical, calm. No curves, no leaves, no bark, no glow blobs, no neon, no stars, no medallions, no text, no UI.
Hell variant
Same model, inverted to paper (#efece7) with black traces; a single red filament (#c2181d) up the trunk, red glow rings at terminals, black triangular shards around the base, faint red ground-plane lines.
References
The founder's Arbor I concept art (green, Hell, and the Yggdrasil III vs Arbor I lens sheet) and the red ground-plane study, on issue 1736; founder/ARBOR-DESIGN.md §3–4; the live prototype scene in this artifact for plane spacing.
Format
Blender source with planes as separate collections; master render 3200 × 2400 PNG; derivatives 1600 × 1200 and 900 × 1200 (mobile) WebP, opaque; a glTF of the trace geometry for the production scene.
Mobile crop
Keep the trunk centred; the mobile crop loses the outer third of each side.
Acceptance
Reads as a tree at thumbnail size and as circuitry up close; never as a rail line or a fence (no long evenly spaced parallels outside the trunk); at 35% opacity under the live graph, bone text in the copy column keeps 7:1.
Terra checks
45° and 90° turns only; front silhouette matches the concept; at least three depth planes visible when the camera orbits 20°; no stars, no rank colour; green used for traces only.

A3 · Observatory interiorEnhancing, optional
Destination
The Observatory page, behind the SVG dome plan.
Objective
Turn the dome diagram into a place: a quiet observatory floor with three apertures throwing their rooms' light.
Prompt for Sol
Looking straight down into the floor of a circular observatory at night, a dark polished stone floor with a fine engraved compass ring. Three long slit apertures in the dome above cast three beams onto the floor at 120 degree intervals: one warm gold, one cool sky blue with a magenta edge, one split prismatic light (violet, blue, cyan, rose). The centre is dark and empty. Architectural, still, scientific. No people, no telescope, no stars painted on surfaces, no text.
References
Skill Heaven site DESIGN.md §05 (one beam, three names); gaia-research brand colours (#38bdf8, #ec4899); Skill Heaven prism hues.
Format
Square master 3000 × 3000; derivative 1600 × 1600 WebP; the beams must sit at 0°, 120° and 240° from the top so the SVG apertures align.
Acceptance
Beam colours match room accents; centre stays clear for the seal; works under a 50% dark overlay.
Terra checks
No figure or silhouette; no lore symbols; alignment within 2° of the SVG apertures.

A5 · Rendered Diamond Seal keystoneNeeded for the Observatory
Destination
The keystone at the centre of The Observatory dome, about 90 to 160 px wide; possibly the 28 px nav seal later.
Geometry
Exactly the canonical Diamond Seal, docs/assets/marks/diamond-seal.svg: one diamond with an EB Garamond SemiBold G. The prototype's inner square is a prototype embellishment and is not requested.
Material and light
Key light from the upper left, soft fill from the lower right; a bevelled metal frame with a centre ridge, the G raised. Yggdrasil: warm gold with parchment highlights and a dark umber recess. Arbor: cooler green-teal metal with a slate recess.
Format
Transparent WebP at 2× for 160, 96 and 56 px, per world; a flat two-tone SVG fallback. No baked background, glow or wordmark.
Acceptance
Overlays diamond-seal.svg within 1 px at 160 px; the diamond and the G stay distinct at 28 px; reads on both world grounds; under 60 KB a file.

Not requested: the Hell-Heaven stamp artwork, which stays a placeholder until the stamps are ratified.
